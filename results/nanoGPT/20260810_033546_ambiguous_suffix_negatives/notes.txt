# Title: Ambiguous Suffix Negatives for Long-Context Disambiguation
# Experiment description: Before training each dataset, build a train-only ambiguous suffix table from train.bin. For suffix lengths such as 4 and 8, map each observed suffix to its most frequent next-character continuations, and keep only suffixes with at least two distinct continuations. Keep the GPT architecture, optimizer, validation, generation, and ordinary next-character cross-entropy unchanged. In the training loop, after warmup_iters and every suffix_negative_interval iterations, sample a bounded number of batch-time positions. For each position, look up the longest available suffix ending at the current input token, choose up to k locally plausible alternative continuations from the suffix table excluding the true target, and compute an auxiliary candidate-set contrastive loss over the full-context logits: cross-entropy where the candidates are the true next token plus the sampled suffix-matched negatives. Train with total_loss = next_token_loss + alpha_t * suffix_negative_loss, ramping alpha_t after warmup. Keep estimate_loss and final reporting based on ordinary validation cross-entropy. Add diagnostics for suffix-table coverage, valid negative positions per batch, candidate-set accuracy, validation loss stratified by ambiguous versus unambiguous suffixes, cropped-context validation losses at 64 and 128 tokens, total train time, inference tokens per second, and generated samples. Compare the baseline against suffix lengths [4], [8], and [4,8], negative counts k in {1,4}, and contrast weights 0.02, 0.05, and 0.1 across shakespeare_char, enwik8, and text8.
