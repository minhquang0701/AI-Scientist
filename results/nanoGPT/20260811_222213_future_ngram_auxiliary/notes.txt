# Title: Future N-Gram Auxiliary Supervision for Tokenizer-Free Character Language Models
# Experiment description: Before training each dataset, scan train.bin once to build a compact vocabulary of frequent future n-grams of length 2 to 6, capped at 1024 or 2048 entries and excluding low-support chunks. Optionally precompute train and validation chunk-label arrays where each position is labeled by the longest frequent n-gram beginning at the next character, with -1 for unsupported positions. Modify GPT.forward() to optionally return final hidden states after ln_f, and add an auxiliary chunk_head Linear(n_embd, chunk_vocab_size) used only during training and diagnostics. Modify get_batch() to return ordinary X, Y plus aligned chunk_labels. In the training loop, always optimize ordinary next-character cross-entropy; after warmup_iters, every chunk_interval iterations such as 4 or 8, add a ramped auxiliary cross-entropy loss on valid chunk_labels with a small maximum weight such as 0.05 to 0.15. Keep evaluation and generation unchanged by using only the standard lm_head for next-character prediction. Extend estimate_loss() to report ordinary train and validation loss as the primary metric, plus chunk-label accuracy, validation loss on frequent-chunk-start positions versus other positions, train-val gap, total train time, and inference tokens per second. Compare against the baseline and a random chunk-label control with the same label frequency distribution across shakespeare_char, enwik8, and text8.
