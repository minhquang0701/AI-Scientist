# Title: Suffix-State Convergence for Prefix-Robust Character Language Modeling
# Experiment description: Modify GPT.forward() to optionally return final hidden states after ln_f. Keep ordinary next-token cross-entropy training unchanged on every iteration. After warmup_iters, every convergence_interval iterations, construct a prefix-replaced view of the current batch by swapping only the older prefix across batch elements while keeping a shared recent suffix of length S, such as 64 or 128, unchanged. Run the clean and prefix-replaced batches with return_hidden=True. Add a small ramped auxiliary loss on the later part of the shared suffix, after a burn-in of 16 or 32 tokens, that encourages normalized hidden states from matched-suffix pairs to be closer than hidden states from unrelated shuffled suffix pairs by a margin. Use detached targets for one branch or a symmetric stop-gradient formulation, and keep the ordinary LM loss as the dominant objective. Extend estimate_loss() to report ordinary train/validation loss, suffix-state convergence distance under prefix replacement, matched-versus-random state margin, validation loss after random prefix replacement, loss by distance into the shared suffix, train-val gap, total train time, and inference tokens per second. Compare against the baseline, absolute hidden-state consistency, and a random-pair consistency control across shakespeare_char, enwik8, and text8. The goal is to test whether encouraging predictive states to become prefix-invariant after enough shared recent context improves boundary robustness and generalization without changing generation or adding inference-time cost.
