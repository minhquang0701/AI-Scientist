# Title: Multi-Scale Boundary Forecasting for Tokenizer-Free Language Models
# Experiment description: After loading each dataset meta.pkl, derive boundary token ids from available whitespace, newline, tab, and common punctuation characters. Modify get_batch() on auxiliary iterations to sample block_size + max_boundary_horizon + 1 contiguous tokens, returning ordinary X and Y plus multi-horizon binary labels for each position indicating whether any boundary token appears within future horizons such as 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 characters. Modify GPT.forward() to optionally return final hidden states after ln_f, and add a small boundary_forecast_head Linear(n_embd, num_horizons) used only during training and diagnostics. Train with ordinary next-character cross-entropy on every iteration; after warmup_iters, every boundary_interval iterations, add a ramped BCEWithLogits auxiliary loss from the forecast head to the multi-horizon labels with a small maximum weight such as 0.05 to 0.15. Keep evaluation and generation through the normal lm_head unchanged. Extend estimate_loss() to report ordinary train and validation loss as the primary metric, boundary forecast BCE or AUROC by horizon, validation loss binned by distance to the next boundary, validation loss near boundaries versus inside spans, generated span-length statistics, total train time, and inference tokens per second. Compare against the baseline, a shuffled-boundary-label control, and a next-character-boundary-only auxiliary control across shakespeare_char, enwik8, and text8.
