# Title: Next-Segment Sketch Planning for Tokenizer-Free Character LMs
# Experiment description: After loading each dataset meta.pkl, derive separator token ids from available whitespace and punctuation, falling back to space-only when appropriate. Modify GPT.forward() to optionally return final hidden states after ln_f, and add two training-only heads: segment_sketch_head Linear(n_embd, sketch_dim) and segment_length_head Linear(n_embd, num_length_buckets). Add compute_next_segment_sketch_targets(chunk, separator_ids), where auxiliary batches sample block_size plus a small future margin and reconstruct chunk = torch.cat([X[:, :1], Y, future_tokens], dim=1). For positions where the visible token is a separator and the next token begins a non-separator segment, collect the next complete segment up to a cap such as 32 characters, build a deterministic normalized signed hash sketch from its character and character-bigram counts, and assign a coarse length bucket; mask all invalid positions. Train every iteration with ordinary next-character cross-entropy. After warmup_iters and every segment_interval iterations, request hidden states from the normal forward pass and add a ramped auxiliary loss combining sketch MSE with length-bucket cross-entropy, with lambda_segment around 0.05 to 0.15. Evaluation and generation ignore the auxiliary heads and use the standard lm_head. Extend estimate_loss() to report ordinary train/validation loss as the primary metric, sketch prediction error, length accuracy, validation loss on segment starts versus inside segments, generated segment-length statistics, train-val gap, total train time, and unchanged inference tokens per second. Compare against the baseline, boundary-forecast-only supervision, length-only supervision, shuffled-sketch controls, and current-segment sketch prediction across shakespeare_char, enwik8, and text8.
