# Title: Blackwell Context-Dominance Regularization for Non-Interfering Long-Context Language Models
# Experiment description: Keep the GPT architecture, data loader, optimizer, ordinary next-character cross-entropy objective, evaluation, and generation path unchanged. After loading each dataset, scan train.bin once to compute unigram frequencies and create fixed unigram-balanced random binary event matrices, with one bank for training and a disjoint bank for diagnostics, using num_events such as 16 or 32. In the training loop, always compute ordinary full-context logits and CE on X,Y. After warmup_iters and every dominance_interval iterations, run one additional short-context forward on X[:, -L:], Y[:, -L:] with L such as 64 or 128, and align the final L positions of the full-context logits with the short-context logits. Add an event_context_dominance_loss helper that converts logits to probabilities, sums probability mass for each binary event, derives event labels from the true targets, and computes binary cross-entropy for full and short contexts. Add a small ramped hinge penalty mean(relu(full_event_bce - detach(short_event_bce) - margin)), so full-context predictions are discouraged from being worse than short-context predictions on coarse predictive decisions without directly distilling the short distribution. Extend estimate_loss to report ordinary validation loss at context lengths 32, 64, 128, and 256, train-bank and held-out-bank event dominance violation rates, mean full-versus-short event regret, true-token negative-context-interference rate, expected calibration error, train-val gap, total train time, and unchanged full-context generation tokens per second. Compare against the baseline, true-token matched-context monotonicity, full-to-short KL consistency, and shuffled event-label controls across shakespeare_char, enwik8, and text8.
